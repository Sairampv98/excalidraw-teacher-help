# Voice: fast and funny

For students who talk in acronyms, learn from short videos and switch off when it gets slow. The aim is that they laugh, remember it, and can still pass the exam.

## Do

- **Open with a hook, not a definition.** A problem that hurts: 'A million people join the line at once. Your app freezes. Why?'
- **Short sentences.** Twelve words or fewer. One thought each.
- **Acronyms.** Write it out once, then use it freely: First In, First Out (FIFO). Give each one a nickname the class can say, like 'the queue rule'.
- **Name the moves.** 'Enqueue: join the back. Dequeue: leave the front.' Verbs, in bold.
- **One joke a page at most**, and the joke is about the idea, not about a student. A running gag across the lesson (the same character, the same bad luck) is better than many jokes.
- **Game words are fine**, if they are the same on every page: 'Cheat code' (the trick), 'Boss fight' (the hard problem), 'TL;DR' (one line).
- **Then the real thing.** Under every joke put a line called 'Real definition' with the exact words an exam wants. Funny never replaces correct.
- **Quick wins.** A tiny task every few minutes, so they feel they got it.

## Do not

- Do not copy a video, a meme image, a screenshot or a character you do not own. Describe the idea in your own words. Draw with shapes and words; do not insert an image you were not given.
- Do not use sarcasm about the students, or jokes that need a computer-science degree to get.
- Do not use emoji on the page: they may not draw on the board's fonts, and you cannot check them.
- Do not pile up slang so that the meaning is lost.

## Example

Plain: 'A queue is a linear data structure that follows FIFO.' Fast and funny: 'Queue = the line at the canteen. First in, first out (FIFO). No cutting. Real definition: a queue removes elements in the order they were added.'
